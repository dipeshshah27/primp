Overnight olive oil focaccia

A wet dough, a cold night in the fridge and four folds. No kneading, no mixer.

By Inés Marlowe · Tested 14 times · ★★★★★ 4.7 (1,902)
  • Hands-on20 min
  • Rest12–18 h
  • Bake25 min
  • Makes1 tray

Watch: the four folds in ninety seconds

Filmed in our test kitchen. The dough in the video is at 80% hydration, the same as this recipe.

Ingredients

Trays 1
  • 500 g strong white bread flour
  • 400 g water, lukewarm
  • 10 g fine sea salt
  • 4 g instant dried yeast
  • 60 g extra virgin olive oil
  • 5 g flaky salt, to finish
  • 8 g rosemary needles

Method

  1. Mix

    Stir the flour, water, salt and yeast in a large bowl until no dry flour remains. The dough will look rough and far too wet. Cover and leave for 30 minutes.

  2. Fold

    With a wet hand, lift one edge of the dough, stretch it up and fold it over the middle. Turn the bowl a quarter and repeat four times. Do this three more times, half an hour apart.

  3. Chill

    Pour half the oil over the dough, turn to coat, cover tightly and refrigerate for 12 to 18 hours.

  4. Shape and proof

    Tip the cold dough into an oiled 23 × 33 cm tray. Let it relax, then ease it into the corners. Leave somewhere warm until puffy and doubled, about 2 hours.

  5. Dimple and bake

    Heat the oven to 230°C. Oil your fingers and press straight down to the tray all over the dough. Scatter with rosemary and flaky salt, pour over the remaining oil, and bake for 22 to 25 minutes until deep gold.

Cook's note. If the dough springs back when you stretch it into the tray, walk away for ten minutes. It is not being difficult, it is resting.
