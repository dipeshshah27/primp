Harbour dredging delayed a third time as silt survey widens

The port authority now expects the main channel to reopen to deep-draught vessels in June, two months later than promised in January.

Marit Oyelaran, shipping correspondent
Updated 09:41:07 · 5 min read
–

Work to deepen the approach channel will not finish before the summer timetable begins, the port authority confirmed on Wednesday night, after surveyors found a second bank of compacted silt north of the breakwater.

The delay is the third since the contract was signed. Two of the four ferry operators that use the harbour said they would keep their larger vessels on the northern route until the channel is certified, adding roughly forty minutes to each crossing.

Harbour master Tomas Edevane told a public meeting that the new bank had not appeared on the 2019 chart and was "almost certainly" laid down by the winter storms. Removing it means bringing a second dredger from across the bay, which the contractor says cannot arrive before the middle of April.

Fishing crews, who can still leave on a rising tide, were less patient. Several skippers said the temporary buoys marking the shallow water had moved twice in a fortnight and that the notice to mariners was out of date within days of being printed.

We were told Easter, then May. Now it is June, if the weather holds. Ruth Calloway, skipper of the Second Wind

The authority said the extra work would cost about a fifth more than the original budget and that it would ask the council for a bridging loan rather than raise berthing fees mid-season. A decision is expected at the finance committee on the 24th.
